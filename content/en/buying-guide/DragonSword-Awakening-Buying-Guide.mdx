DragonSword: Awakening represents a rare proposition in the modern RPG market: a full-priced buy-to-play game with no gacha mechanics, no pay-to-win elements, and all 19 heroes included in the base purchase. At $29.99, it is positioned as an affordable entry point compared to $60-70 AAA titles, while delivering a complete experience that does not require additional spending to access all gameplay content. However, understanding exactly what you get for your money — and what the optional DLC provides — is important before making your purchase decision.

This guide covers every aspect of buying DragonSword: Awakening, from the available editions and their contents to the DLC Familiar model and how it compares to the monetization systems of competing games. Whether you are deciding between the Standard and Deluxe editions or wondering whether the game is truly free of gacha mechanics, this guide provides the answers.

## Pricing and Available Editions

DragonSword: Awakening is available on Steam with two purchase options: the Standard Edition and the Deluxe Edition. Both editions include the complete base game with all 19 heroes, all dungeons, and all gameplay content. The only differences are in bonus Familiar companions and cosmetic items.

### Standard Edition — $29.99

The Standard Edition includes the complete DragonSword: Awakening experience:

- All 19 playable heroes at launch

- Full story campaign across the Continent of Orbis

- All dungeon content (Standard, Hard, and Very Hard)

- The Rift System for endgame progression

- 20 free Familiars (2/3 of the total 30 Familiars)

- Full tag-team combat system with all 5 Status Ailments

- All cooking and crafting systems

- 11 supported languages

The Standard Edition provides the full gameplay experience with no content locked behind additional purchases. You can access every dungeon, use every hero, and progress through all content without spending more than the initial $29.99. This is a core design principle of the game and one that distinguishes it from competitors that gate heroes or content behind gacha mechanics.

### Deluxe Edition — $39.99

The Deluxe Edition includes everything in the Standard Edition plus:

- **Valiant Hatchling Familiar** — A cosmetic Familiar companion with the Valiant Hatchling skin

- **Exclusive weapon skin set** — Cosmetic weapon skins for all 19 heroes

- **Digital soundtrack** — The game's original soundtrack in MP3 format

- **Digital art book** — A collection of concept art and character designs

The Deluxe Edition costs $10 more than the Standard Edition. The primary gameplay addition is the Valiant Hatchling Familiar, which is important to understand correctly: it provides the same type of passive bonuses as free Familiars, not unique or superior bonuses. The weapon skins and digital content are purely cosmetic and supplementary. For a detailed analysis of whether the Deluxe Edition is worth the upgrade, see our [Deluxe edition guide](/en/buying-guide/deluxe-edition-guide/).

## The No-Gacha Model: What It Means for Players

The single most important thing to understand about DragonSword: Awakening's monetization is that it contains absolutely no gacha mechanics. This is not a "mostly no gacha" situation — there are no loot boxes, no random hero pulls, no random weapon drops from paid spins, and no premium currency used for gambling-style mechanics. Every hero is earned through the deterministic Hero Request system, every weapon is crafted or obtained as a fixed dungeon drop, and every Familiar is either included in the base game or available as a specific DLC purchase.

### Why This Matters

The original Korean version of DragonSword was a mobile gacha game published by Webzen. When Hound13 decided to create a PC version, they completely rebuilt the monetization model from scratch, removing all gacha elements and converting to a buy-to-play structure. This transition was widely covered in gaming media and is a significant reason for the game's positive community reception. Over 200,000 players wishlisted the game on Steam before launch, with many citing the no-gacha model as their primary interest.

For players coming from gacha RPGs like Genshin Impact or Honkai: Star Rail, the difference is dramatic. In those games, obtaining a top-tier hero requires either significant spending or months of grinding for premium currency. In DragonSword: Awakening, all 19 heroes are available to every player who purchases the game — no random draws, no pity systems, no duplicate shards. The only "grind" is completing Hero Request questlines, which are story-driven activities that you would be doing anyway.

For more on this topic, see our dedicated [no gacha explained guide](/en/buying-guide/no-gacha-explained/).

## DLC Familiar Model Explained

While the base game includes 20 free Familiars, 10 additional Familiars are available as paid DLC. Understanding how this system works is critical because it is the only post-purchase spending option in the game.

### DLC Familiars Are Cosmetic, Not Power

The most important fact about DLC Familiars is that they provide the same type of passive bonuses as free Familiars. A DLC Familiar does not offer unique bonuses that are unavailable through free Familiars — it offers a different Familiar skin with comparable passive effects. This means DLC Familiars are cosmetic and collection-oriented, not power-oriented.

For example, the Valiant Hatchling (Deluxe Edition) provides a passive attack power bonus similar to the Inferno Drake (free Familiar). The Abyssal Direwolf (launch bonus) provides a passive ailment application bonus similar to the Frost Wisp (free Familiar). The specific numbers may differ slightly, but no DLC Familiar provides a bonus that cannot be matched or exceeded by a free Familiar with the right pairing.

## What Your Purchase Does NOT Include

To set proper expectations, here is what your $29.99 Standard Edition purchase does not include:

- **Post-launch heroes**: Hound13 has announced four additional heroes (Liza, Jerome, Veronica, Logan) coming in free updates. These are not included at launch but will be free for all players when they release.

- **Additional Familiars beyond the 20 free ones**: The 10 DLC Familiars require separate purchase, though none provide unique gameplay advantages.

- **Future expansions**: If Hound13 releases paid story expansions, these would be separate purchases. No such expansions have been announced.

- **Cosmetic weapon skins**: Beyond the Deluxe Edition skin set, additional cosmetic skins may be sold separately. These do not affect gameplay.

## Regional Pricing and Payment Options

DragonSword: Awakening is priced at $29.99 USD for the Standard Edition on Steam. Regional pricing varies based on Steam's regional pricing algorithm, which adjusts prices for different territories based on purchasing power. In South Korea, the game is priced at approximately 33,000 KRW, which is roughly equivalent to $24-25 USD at current exchange rates. Japanese pricing is approximately 3,600 JPY ($23-24 USD equivalent). These regional adjustments make the game more accessible in markets with lower average incomes.

Steam supports a wide variety of payment methods including credit cards, PayPal, and regional options like AliPay and Konbini payment. The game is available for purchase immediately on the Steam store page, and pre-loading was available before the July 22 launch date. Purchasing the game activates it in your Steam library permanently, and the game is yours to keep even if you uninstall and reinstall later.

### Steam Refund Policy

DragonSword: Awakening is covered by Steam's standard refund policy. If you have played the game for less than 2 hours and owned it for less than 14 days, you can request a full refund through the Steam support page. Two hours is enough time to complete the tutorial and experience the opening combat encounters, giving you a fair opportunity to evaluate whether the tag combo system and overall gameplay meet your expectations. If the game does not perform well on your hardware, the refund policy also covers performance-related refund requests within the same time limits.

## FAQ

### Is DragonSword Awakening worth the price?

At $29.99 for the complete game with all 19 heroes, no gacha, and all gameplay content, DragonSword: Awakening offers exceptional value compared to both premium single-player RPGs and free-to-play gacha games. For a detailed analysis, see our [is it worth buying guide](/en/buying-guide/is-it-worth-buying/).

### Do I need to buy the Deluxe Edition to get all Familiars?

No, the Deluxe Edition only provides the Valiant Hatchling Familiar. The 20 free Familiars in the base game provide comparable passive bonuses, and the additional 10 DLC Familiars are available as separate purchases. You do not need the Deluxe Edition for gameplay completeness — it is a cosmetic and collection upgrade.

### Can I upgrade from Standard to Deluxe later?

Yes, Steam allows you to upgrade from the Standard Edition to the Deluxe Edition by purchasing the upgrade DLC. The upgrade costs the $10 difference between editions. All Deluxe content (Valiant Hatchling, weapon skins, soundtrack, art book) is added to your account immediately upon purchase.

### Will there be a sale or discount?

As a newly launched game, DragonSword: Awakening is unlikely to see a sale within the first 3-6 months. Hound13 has not announced any discount plans. The $29.99 price point is already positioned below typical AAA pricing, which suggests the launch price is the intended value proposition. If you are waiting for a sale, the earliest likely discount would be a Steam seasonal sale (Autumn or Winter 2026).

### Is the game available on platforms other than Steam?

DragonSword: Awakening is currently available exclusively on Steam for PC. Hound13 has not announced console versions, though the UE5 engine makes console ports technically feasible. The legal dispute with former publisher Webzen may complicate platform expansion. For the latest platform availability, check the [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4570720/DragonSword__Awakening/).
